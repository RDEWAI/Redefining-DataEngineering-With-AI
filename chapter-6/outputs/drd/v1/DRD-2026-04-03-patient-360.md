## Executive Summary

The Patient 360 Search initiative delivers a unified search interface enabling authorized clinical and administrative staff to retrieve a complete patient view in under 2 seconds. By consolidating data from the Synthea Healthcare EHR database — covering 5,767 patients (5,000 active), 340,532 encounters, and 4.3M+ observations — the initiative eliminates the current 8-minute multi-system lookup workflow. Phase 1 focuses exclusively on the clinical data foundation (patients, encounters, conditions, medications, observations, allergies, immunizations, careplans, and procedures). Financial data integration is deferred to Phase 2. Success is measured by 90% of searches returning complete results within 2 seconds, zero missed allergy alerts, and a 50% reduction in pre-appointment chart review time.

## 1. Business Context

### 1.1 Business Request

Clinicians currently navigate three to five separate systems to piece together a complete picture of a patient before each appointment. On average, a physician spends 8 minutes per patient just finding and cross-referencing information across these systems. This fragmented workflow leads to clinician frustration, missed critical information (allergies, recent lab results), inefficient care coordination, and excessive time reconciling encounter and billing data.

The Patient 360 Search provides a single search interface where any authorized staff member can type a patient's name and instantly see a consolidated view including demographics, recent encounters, active conditions, current medications, lab results, known allergies, and immunization history.

### 1.2 Business Objectives

1. Reduce clinician time spent on patient lookups from 8 minutes to under 30 seconds
2. Eliminate missed allergy alerts by surfacing them prominently in every patient record
3. Enable care coordinators to review 30+ patients per day (up from 10–15 currently)
4. Provide billing staff with instant access to encounter-claims reconciliation (Phase 2)
5. Support quality reporting by making readmission data easily accessible

### 1.3 Success Criteria

- 90% of searches return complete results within 2 seconds (measured at p90 via synthetic load testing)
- Clinician satisfaction score above 4.0 out of 5.0 in post-launch survey
- 50% reduction in pre-appointment chart review time
- Zero missed allergy alerts in the first 90 days post-launch
- All active patients (~5,000 per actual database count) searchable from day one
- System availability ≥ 99.5% during business hours (6 AM – 10 PM)

## 5. Business Rules

### 5.1 Default Values

| Field | Default Value | When Applied | Business Justification |
|-------|---------------|--------------|------------------------|
| allergies.severity1 | "Unknown" (display only) | severity1 IS NULL | 56.4% of allergy records lack severity; display must never be suppressed — patient safety |
| encounter cost component | $0.00 | Any of base_encounter_cost, procedures.base_cost, medications.totalcost IS NULL | Enables cost calculation to proceed; prevents NULL propagation in totals |
| patients.marital (display) | "Not recorded" | marital IS NULL | No clinical significance; avoids blank UI elements |
| medication status display | "Discontinued" | stop IS NOT NULL | Explicitly stopped medications are discontinued; active = stop IS NULL |

### 5.2 Calculations and Derivations

#### Patient Age

- **Formula / Logic**: `DATE_DIFF('year', patients.birthdate, CURRENT_DATE)`, adjusted for whether birthday has occurred yet in the current year
- **Input Fields**: `patients.birthdate`, `CURRENT_DATE`
- **Output Field**: `patient_age_years` (integer)
- **Business Purpose**: Display age in search results per Dr. Amy Nguyen's requirement (name, age, last visit date in result list)
- **Example**: birthdate = 1970-06-15, query date = 2026-04-03 → age = 55

#### Last Visit Date

- **Formula / Logic**: `MAX(encounters.start)` per patient
- **Input Fields**: `encounters.start`, grouped by `encounters.patient`
- **Output Field**: `last_visit_date` (DATE)
- **Business Purpose**: Show in search result list before opening full profile
- **Example**: Patient with encounters on 2024-01-10, 2024-08-15, 2025-11-20 → last_visit_date = 2025-11-20

#### Total Visit Cost

- **Formula / Logic**: `COALESCE(base_encounter_cost, 0) + COALESCE(SUM(procedures.base_cost), 0) + COALESCE(SUM(medications.totalcost), 0)` per encounter
- **Input Fields**: `encounters.base_encounter_cost`, `procedures.base_cost`, `medications.totalcost`
- **Output Field**: `total_visit_cost` (DOUBLE, USD)
- **Business Purpose**: Pre-calculated billing summary per encounter for billing staff (Phase 2 display; calculation defined in Phase 1)
- **Example**: Encounter base cost = $150.00, 2 procedures at $75.00 and $NULL, 1 medication at $25.00 → total = $150 + $75 + $0 + $25 = $250.00

#### 30-Day Readmission Flag

- **Formula / Logic**: For each inpatient encounter (`encounterclass = 'inpatient'`), check whether the same patient had another inpatient encounter within 30 days prior to this encounter's `start` date
- **Input Fields**: `encounters.patient`, `encounters.start`, `encounters.encounterclass`
- **Output Field**: `is_readmission` (BOOLEAN)
- **Business Purpose**: Quality reporting metric; care coordinators and CMO need readmission identification
- **Example**: Patient has inpatient encounter on 2025-03-01 and another on 2025-03-20 → the 2025-03-20 encounter is flagged `is_readmission = TRUE` (19 days after prior discharge)
- **Scope**: Inpatient encounters only (5,597 in database). `encounterclass` must equal `'inpatient'`.

#### Active Medication

- **Formula / Logic**: `medications.stop IS NULL`
- **Input Fields**: `medications.stop`
- **Output Field**: `is_active` (BOOLEAN)
- **Business Purpose**: Distinguish active from discontinued medications per Dr. Amy Nguyen's request
- **Example**: Medication with stop = NULL → active; medication with stop = 2024-06-01 → discontinued

### 5.3 Transformation Rules

- **Patient display name**: Concatenate `COALESCE(prefix, '') || ' ' || first || ' ' || last || COALESCE(' ' || suffix, '')`, trimmed of leading/trailing whitespace
- **SSN masking**: Show only last 4 digits for disambiguation: `'***-**-' || SUBSTRING(ssn, 8, 4)`. Never expose full SSN in any display layer.
- **Allergy severity display**: Map `severity1` values to title case: `'MILD' → 'Mild'`, `'MODERATE' → 'Moderate'`, `'SEVERE' → 'Severe'`, `NULL → 'Unknown'`
- **Encounter class display**: Capitalize encounter class for display: `'ambulatory' → 'Ambulatory'`, `'inpatient' → 'Inpatient'`, etc.
- **Observation value normalization**: Observations store values as VARCHAR. Numeric values should be cast to DOUBLE for range comparisons; non-numeric values (e.g., "Positive", "Negative") are stored as-is.
- **Deceased patient flag**: When `patients.deathdate IS NOT NULL`, prepend a "Deceased" indicator to the patient record with `deathdate` displayed.

### 5.4 Edge Cases and Exceptions

- **Scenario**: Patient name search returns multiple matches (e.g., "John Smith")
  - **Expected Behavior**: Return all matches; display name, age, DOB, last 4 of SSN, and last visit date to enable disambiguation
  - **Rationale**: Dr. Amy Nguyen identified this as a real workflow issue; SSN last 4 is the standard clinical disambiguation method

- **Scenario**: Patient has no encounter history
  - **Expected Behavior**: Display patient demographics only; show "No encounter history" in the encounter section
  - **Rationale**: New patients or patients transferred from another system may have demographics without encounter records

- **Scenario**: Allergy record has no severity
  - **Expected Behavior**: Display the allergy with severity shown as "Unknown" — never hide the record
  - **Rationale**: A hidden allergy is a patient safety risk; 56.4% of records are affected

- **Scenario**: Deceased patient
  - **Expected Behavior**: Include in search results with a prominent "Deceased" label and `deathdate` displayed
  - **Rationale**: Deceased patient records are still needed for clinical, legal, and administrative purposes

- **Scenario**: Patient with no active medications
  - **Expected Behavior**: Show "No active medications" rather than an empty list
  - **Rationale**: Clinical staff must distinguish "no medications" from "medications not loaded"

- **Scenario**: Patient with no recorded allergies
  - **Expected Behavior**: Show "No known allergies (NKA)" — not a blank field
  - **Rationale**: A blank allergy field is ambiguous; "NKA" is the clinical standard when no allergies are on record

- **Scenario**: Multiple encounter cost components are NULL
  - **Expected Behavior**: Sum non-NULL components; treat NULL as $0; do not flag the visit cost as incomplete
  - **Rationale**: Stakeholder decision — cost completeness is a Phase 2 billing concern

## 6. Assumptions and Open Questions

### 6.1 Assumptions

1. The Synthea DuckDB database at `data/duckdb/raw.db` (repo root) is the single source of truth for all Phase 1 data. No other source systems are in scope.
2. Financial data (claims, payers, billing summary) is deferred to Phase 2. Billing staff requirements are out of scope for this DRD.
3. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) design is deferred to Phase 2. Phase 1 assumes read-only analytics access with no per-user data filtering.
4. The search interface (UI) is out of Phase 1 scope. This DRD covers the data foundation only.
5. Active patient is defined as a patient with `deathdate IS NULL` (5,000 records as of 2026-04-03).
6. Active medication is defined as a medication with `stop IS NULL`. No 90-day recency window applies.
7. Daily batch load from CSV source files is the established refresh mechanism. No streaming or CDC is planned for Phase 1.
8. NULL cost fields in encounters, procedures, and medications are treated as $0.00 in total visit cost calculations.
9. The database size (~580 MB) is well within local DuckDB capacity; no partitioning or archiving is needed for Phase 1.
10. Encounter history display shows all encounters; "last 12 months" from the business request is a UI filter preference, not a data truncation rule.

## 7. Regulatory and Compliance

### 7.1 Applicable Regulations

| Regulation | Scope | Key Requirements | Impact on Data Design |
|------------|-------|------------------|-----------------------|
| HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Part 164) | All patient data in the Patient 360 system | Minimum necessary access; patient right to access records; prohibition on disclosure without authorization | All PHI access (clinical, demographic, encounter records) must be role-based; no bulk export without authorization; access logs required |
| HIPAA Security Rule | Electronic PHI (ePHI) stored and accessed | Administrative, physical, and technical safeguards; access controls; audit controls | Encryption at rest and in transit for production; audit logging for all ePHI access; RBAC enforcement |

### 7.2 Data Classification

| Data Element | Classification Level | Handling Requirements |
|-------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Patient demographics (name, birthdate, address, SSN) | PHI + PII — Highly Sensitive | Encrypt at rest; SSN must be masked to last 4 in all display layers; never log full SSN |
| Clinical records (diagnoses, medications, lab results, allergies) | PHI — Highly Sensitive | Role-based access; encrypt at rest and in transit; access logging required |
| Encounter history (dates, types, costs) | PHI — Sensitive | Role-based access; cost data visible to billing roles only (Phase 2) |
| Care plan and procedure records | PHI — Sensitive | Role-based access; audit logging required |
| Immunization records | PHI — Sensitive | Role-based access; shareable with public health authorities per HIPAA exceptions |
| De-identified aggregate metrics (counts, rates) | Internal | No PHI; can be shared within the organization without restriction |

### 7.3 Retention Requirements

| Data Category | Retention Period | Deletion Method | Legal Basis |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Adult patient clinical records | 7 years from last encounter | Secure deletion with audit trail | HIPAA minimum; state law default |
| Minor patient records | Until patient reaches age 25 or 7 years (whichever is longer) | Secure deletion with audit trail | Standard healthcare regulation |
| Deceased patient records | 7 years from date of death | Secure deletion with audit trail | HIPAA + estate/legal requirements |
| Audit logs (access, modification) | 6 years | Archival then secure deletion | HIPAA Security Rule (45 CFR §164.312) |

### 7.4 Access Controls

| Role / Group | Data Access Level | Restrictions | Authentication |
|-------------|-------------------|--------------|----------------|
| Physicians | Full clinical profile (demographics, encounters, conditions, meds, allergies, labs, immunizations) | No financial data (Phase 2); no bulk export | RBAC (Phase 2 design); MFA required |
| Nurses | Clinical profile scoped to allergy and medication views | No financial data; no bulk export; SSN last 4 for disambiguation only | RBAC (Phase 2 design); MFA required |
| Care Coordinators | Clinical summary + readmission flags + care gap indicators | No financial data; panel-level access only (own panel) | RBAC (Phase 2 design); MFA required |
| Department Heads | Aggregate metrics only (de-identified counts and rates) | No individual patient PHI in reporting view | RBAC (Phase 2 design); MFA required |
| Data Engineers (dev) | Read-only DuckDB access during Phase 1 development | Local development only; no production PHI in dev environment | Local file access (dev); RBAC (production) |
| Billing Staff | Financial data only (Phase 2) | Out of Phase 1 scope | RBAC (Phase 2 design) |

### 7.5 Audit Requirements

- **Access Logging**: Every PHI access event (search, record view) must be logged with: user ID, timestamp, patient ID accessed, and access type. Required by HIPAA Security Rule.
- **Modification Audit Trail**: Any change to source data must be tracked with before/after values, user ID, and timestamp.
- **Breach Detection**: Unusual access patterns (bulk exports, off-hours access, high-volume queries) must trigger alerts to the Security Officer.
- **Retention of Audit Logs**: Audit logs must be retained for a minimum of 6 years per HIPAA Security Rule §164.312.
- **Audit Log Integrity**: Audit logs must be write-once; no modification or deletion permitted by any user including database administrators.
