## Executive Summary

This document defines the data requirements for the **Patient 360 Search** initiative, which aims to consolidate patient information from the Synthea Healthcare EHR database into a unified search experience. The solution will enable clinicians, nurses, care coordinators, and billing staff to search for patients by name and instantly view a comprehensive profile including demographics, encounters, conditions, medications, lab results, allergies, and billing information.

The primary goals are to reduce clinician lookup time from 8 minutes to under 30 seconds, eliminate missed allergy alerts, and support 400+ concurrent users with sub-2-second search response times. The data foundation is the existing DuckDB database containing 18 tables with approximately 5,700 patients and 13 million associated records.

## 1. Business Context

### 1.1 Business Request

Clinicians currently navigate three to five separate systems to piece together a complete picture of a patient before each appointment. On average, a physician spends 8 minutes per patient just finding and cross-referencing information across systems. This fragmented workflow creates clinician frustration, missed critical information (allergies, recent lab results), inefficient care coordination, and excessive time spent on billing reconciliation.

The desired outcome is a **Patient 360 experience** — a single search interface where any authorized staff member can type a patient's name and instantly see a complete, consolidated view of that patient's demographics, encounters, conditions, medications, lab results, allergies, and billing summary.

### 1.2 Business Objectives

- Reduce clinician time spent on patient lookups from 8 minutes to under 30 seconds
- Eliminate missed allergy alerts by surfacing them prominently in every search result
- Enable care coordinators to review 30+ patients per day (up from 10-15 currently)
- Provide billing staff with instant access to encounter-claims reconciliation
- Support quality reporting by making readmission data easily accessible

### 1.3 Success Criteria

- 90% of searches return complete results within 2 seconds
- Clinician satisfaction score above 4.0 out of 5.0 in post-launch survey
- 50% reduction in pre-appointment chart review time
- Zero missed allergy alerts in the first 90 days
- All active patients (~5,700 in current system) searchable from day one

## 5. Business Rules

### 5.1 Default Values

| Field | Default Value | When Applied | Business Justification |
|-------|---------------|--------------|------------------------|
| allergy.severity | 'UNKNOWN' | When severity1 is NULL | Clinicians must still be alerted; unknown severity is not safe to ignore |
| medication.status | 'ACTIVE' | When stop date is NULL or in the future | Medications without end date are presumed ongoing |
| patient.age | Calculated from birthdate | Always | Age is more clinically relevant than birthdate alone |
| encounter.total_cost | base_encounter_cost | When total_claim_cost is NULL | Provides baseline cost estimate for billing |

### 5.2 Calculations and Derivations

#### Patient Age

- **Formula / Logic**: `FLOOR((CURRENT_DATE - birthdate) / 365.25)` in years
- **Input Fields**: patients.birthdate
- **Output Field**: Derived field: patient_age
- **Business Purpose**: Display age in search results and patient profile for clinical context
- **Example**: birthdate = 1985-03-15, today = 2026-02-10 → age = 40

#### Total Visit Cost

- **Formula / Logic**: `SUM(encounters.base_encounter_cost) + SUM(procedures.base_cost) + SUM(medications.totalcost)` for a given encounter
- **Input Fields**: encounters.base_encounter_cost, procedures.base_cost, medications.totalcost
- **Output Field**: Derived field: total_visit_cost
- **Business Purpose**: Provide billing staff with pre-calculated cost per encounter for reconciliation
- **Example**: Encounter cost $150 + Procedure $75 + Medication $25 = Total $250

#### Active Medication Status

- **Formula / Logic**: `IF(stop IS NULL OR stop > CURRENT_DATE, 'Active', 'Discontinued')`
- **Input Fields**: medications.start, medications.stop
- **Output Field**: Derived field: medication_status
- **Business Purpose**: Distinguish current medications from historical prescriptions
- **Example**: start = 2025-01-01, stop = NULL → 'Active'

#### 30-Day Readmission Flag

- **Formula / Logic**: For each inpatient encounter, check if another inpatient encounter for the same patient starts within 30 days of the previous discharge
- **Input Fields**: encounters.patient, encounters.encounterclass, encounters.start, encounters.stop
- **Output Field**: Derived field: is_readmission (boolean)
- **Business Purpose**: Quality reporting and care coordination for post-discharge follow-up
- **Example**: Inpatient discharge 2026-01-01, new inpatient admission 2026-01-20 → is_readmission = true

#### Last Encounter Date

- **Formula / Logic**: `MAX(encounters.start) GROUP BY patient`
- **Input Fields**: encounters.patient, encounters.start
- **Output Field**: Derived field: last_encounter_date
- **Business Purpose**: Show recency of patient activity in search results
- **Example**: Patient has encounters on 2025-06-01, 2025-09-15, 2026-01-10 → last_encounter_date = 2026-01-10

### 5.3 Transformation Rules

- **Name Formatting**: Display patient names as "Last, First" in search results for consistency with clinical convention
- **SSN Masking**: Display only last 4 digits (***-**-1234) for patient disambiguation; never expose full SSN
- **Date Formatting**: Display all dates in MM/DD/YYYY format for US clinical staff
- **Code Description Fallback**: If SNOMED/LOINC/RxNorm description is missing, display the code itself with a "[Code]" suffix

### 5.4 Edge Cases and Exceptions

- **Scenario**: Patient has duplicate records (same name, same birthdate)
  - **Expected Behavior**: Display both records in search results with additional identifiers (last 4 SSN, address) to help user select correct patient
  - **Rationale**: Data steward will resolve duplicates separately; search must not hide potential matches

- **Scenario**: Patient has no encounters in the last 12 months
  - **Expected Behavior**: Still display patient in search results; show "No recent encounters" in the encounter section
  - **Rationale**: Patient may still be active in the system; historical data is still valuable

- **Scenario**: Allergy record has NULL severity
  - **Expected Behavior**: Display allergy with "Severity: Unknown" label; still prominently displayed
  - **Rationale**: Unknown severity does not mean no risk; err on the side of caution

- **Scenario**: Patient has multiple insurance payers (primary and secondary)
  - **Expected Behavior**: Display both payers in the billing summary with labels (Primary, Secondary)
  - **Rationale**: Billing staff need visibility into coordination of benefits

- **Scenario**: Observation value is NULL
  - **Expected Behavior**: Display the observation with "Result pending" or "Not recorded" label
  - **Rationale**: ~2% of observations have NULL values; still show the test was ordered

## 6. Assumptions and Open Questions

### 6.1 Assumptions

- The Synthea DuckDB database is the single source of truth for all patient data in this phase
- Data is refreshed via daily batch load from CSV source files; near-real-time feeds are not available in Phase 1
- Role-based access control (RBAC) will be implemented in Phase 2; Phase 1 focuses on data consolidation
- HIPAA security architecture (encryption, audit logging, access controls) is being addressed in a parallel workstream
- All 5,767 patients in the current database are considered "active" and should be searchable
- The search interface will be web-based; mobile access is out of scope for Phase 1

## 7. Regulatory and Compliance

### 7.1 Applicable Regulations

| Regulation | Scope | Key Requirements | Impact on Data Design |
|------------|-------|------------------|-----------------------|
| HIPAA Privacy Rule | All patient health information (PHI) | Minimum necessary disclosure; patient right to access; authorization for non-treatment uses | Restrict query results to authorized users; implement access logging |
| HIPAA Security Rule | Electronic PHI (ePHI) | Administrative, physical, and technical safeguards; encryption; access controls | Encrypt data at rest and in transit; implement RBAC in Phase 2 |
| HIPAA Breach Notification | All PHI | 60-day notification requirement for breaches affecting 500+ individuals | Implement breach detection and incident response procedures |
| State Medical Records Laws | Patient records | Varies by state; typically 7-10 year retention | Ensure retention capabilities in data architecture |

### 7.2 Data Classification

| Data Element | Classification Level | Handling Requirements |
|-------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Patient SSN | PHI - Highly Sensitive | Mask in display (show only last 4); encrypt at rest; audit all access |
| Patient Name, DOB, Address | PHI - Sensitive | Encrypt at rest; role-based access; audit access |
| Medical Conditions, Medications, Allergies | PHI - Clinical | Encrypt at rest; restrict to clinical roles; audit access |
| Lab Results, Vitals | PHI - Clinical | Encrypt at rest; restrict to clinical roles; audit access |
| Billing/Claims Data | PHI - Financial | Encrypt at rest; restrict to billing and clinical roles; audit access |
| Provider Names, Organization Data | Internal - Business | Standard access controls; no special handling required |

### 7.3 Retention Requirements

| Data Category | Retention Period | Deletion Method | Legal Basis |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Adult patient records | 7 years from last encounter | Secure deletion with audit trail | State medical records law (varies; 7 years is minimum) |
| Minor patient records | Age 21 + 7 years | Secure deletion with audit trail | State medical records law for minors |
| Billing/claims data | 7 years | Secure deletion with audit trail | IRS requirements; payer contracts |
| Audit logs | 6 years | Secure archival then deletion | HIPAA Security Rule |

### 7.4 Access Controls

| Role / Group | Data Access Level | Restrictions | Authentication |
|-------------|-------------------|--------------|----------------|
| Physicians | Full clinical data (demographics, encounters, conditions, meds, allergies, labs) | Limited billing detail | SSO with MFA (Phase 2) |
| Nurses | Clinical data excluding detailed billing | No claims transaction detail | SSO with MFA (Phase 2) |
| Care Coordinators | Full clinical data for assigned patient panels | Panel-restricted access | SSO with MFA (Phase 2) |
| Billing Staff | Demographics, encounters, claims, financial data | Limited clinical detail (diagnosis codes only) | SSO with MFA (Phase 2) |
| Department Heads | Aggregate reports only; no individual patient lookup | No direct patient data access | SSO with MFA (Phase 2) |
| IT Administrators | System metadata and logs | No patient data access | SSO with MFA + privileged access management |

### 7.5 Audit Requirements

- **Access Logging**: Log every patient record access with timestamp, user ID, patient ID, and action type (view, search, export)
- **Query Logging**: Log all SQL queries executed against patient data for forensic analysis
- **Failed Access Attempts**: Log and alert on repeated failed authentication or authorization attempts
- **Data Export Tracking**: Log any bulk data export or download with business justification requirement
- **Periodic Access Review**: Quarterly review of user access rights with manager attestation
